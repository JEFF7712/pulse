# Landing Page Content Design

## Goal

Refocus the Pulse landing page copy around product clarity so visitors quickly understand what Pulse is, what it does, and why they should trust it.

## Current Problems

- The page is visually strong, but the message is still broad and manifesto-heavy.
- The problem statement is clear, but the product behavior stays abstract for too long.
- The `about` and `philosophy` sections repeat the same sovereignty idea instead of progressing the story.
- The signup CTA feels passive and launch-oriented instead of inviting the right early users.

## Approved Direction

### 1. Shift the voice from manifesto to product explanation

Keep the calm, opinionated tone, but make the page read like a credible product page rather than a general anti-surveillance statement. The sovereignty angle remains important, but it should support the product story rather than replace it.

### 2. Make the page flow answer buyer questions in order

The copy should move through this sequence:

1. What is Pulse?
2. What useful things does it help me notice?
3. What sources does it connect to?
4. Why should I trust it?
5. Why should I sign up now?

### 3. Make each section more concrete

- **Hero:** define Pulse directly in plain English and explain the outcome.
- **About:** describe how Pulse behaves and what kinds of observations it surfaces.
- **Connectors:** position data sources as signals that become useful together.
- **Philosophy/Trust:** shorten and focus on self-hosting, readable memory, and user control.
- **Signup:** invite early access for people who want the product first, not just a launch alert.

## Content Principles

- Prefer clear product language over abstract slogans.
- Use 2-3 concrete examples of insights Pulse can generate.
- Reduce repeated anti-corporate rhetoric.
- Keep trust claims specific and inspectable.
- Preserve the existing layout unless copy changes expose a real structural gap.

## Expected Editing Scope

- Rewrite headlines, section intros, and body copy in `site/index.html`.
- Keep the existing visual system and section structure mostly intact.
- Avoid major layout or styling changes unless a copy improvement clearly needs one.
